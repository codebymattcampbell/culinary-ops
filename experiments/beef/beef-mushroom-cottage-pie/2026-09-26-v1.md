## Observations

- Rook used the largest available skillet; it was quite full after adding the 2 lb of beef to the vegetables.

- Filling tasted a little bland. Rook added more Worcestershire sauce and salt before baking; extra amounts were not measured.
- Garlic powder was used; no fresh garlic.
- Mashed potatoes seemed a little dry and were difficult to spread. Some stuck to the spoon and pulled up meat, leaving one corner mixed and uneven.
- Final eating texture is not yet known; difficult spreading does not by itself confirm a dry finished pie.
- Mushrooms were planned for a separate pan so they could brown while the onion, optional carrots, and beef cooked.
- Broccoli was kept out of the pie and reserved as a side.
- Pie was reported about five minutes into baking at 5:40 p.m. MDT.

## Before the Next Cook

- Allow for skillet capacity: set the sautéed vegetables aside, brown the beef in two batches, then return the vegetables and combine. If the filling is still too crowded to stir comfortably, transfer to a larger pot for the gravy.

- Review these notes before shopping, prep, or repeating the recipe.
- Taste and adjust the finished filling before assembly. Start with the recipe amounts, then add salt and Worcestershire to taste; record the final quantities.
- Taste a bite of filling with mashed potato before assembly to judge the combined seasoning.
- Add milk to the mash a little at a time until it spreads easily while still holding its shape. Do not treat the starting milk quantity as a hard limit.
- Apply the mash in small dollops and spread gently with a lightly buttered spoon.
- Keep the separate mushroom-pan option for easier moisture control.
