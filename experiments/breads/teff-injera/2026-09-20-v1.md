# Teff Injera — V1

## Date

20 Sep 2026

## Source Recipe

- [Teff Injera](../../../recipes/ethiopian/breads/teff-injera.md)

## Result

**Partial success — continue experimenting**

The injera was enjoyable and the meal received great reviews, but V1 did not meet the intended injera targets for sourness, open eyes, or a light/spongy structure.

## Formula

- 260 g teff flour
- 475 g water
- 1/4 tsp instant yeast
- About 24 hours room-temperature fermentation
- Absit made from 1/2 cup fermented batter + 1/2 cup water
- 1/2 tsp kosher salt
- 1 tbsp additional water added to the main batter after the first test injera

## Observations

### Fermentation and Flavor

- Batter showed obvious fermentation activity, including bubbles, separation, and evidence of rising and falling.
- Despite that activity, the finished injera had essentially **no sour flavor**.
- Best description of the result: **"wheat, savory pancake."**
- Next test will use a full 48-hour ferment.

### Absit

- The instruction to cook to a **pudding consistency** was understandable and useful.
- The additional cue to wait for the absit to become **"translucent-ish" was confusing and counterproductive**.
- The teff absit appeared opaque throughout cooking, which encouraged continued cooking past the pudding stage.
- It became thick enough to set into a jiggly gel as it cooled.
- Future instruction: **stop at smooth, thick pudding consistency. Do not use translucency or clarity as a required cue.**

### Batter and Cooking

- First injera was too thick. The surface dried and cracked while the center remained gooey.
- Adding 1 tbsp water to the remaining batter improved flow and produced a more even round.
- Most bubbles/eyes remained small and pinprick-like.
- Finished texture was relatively dense, without the larger holes and spongy internal structure desired.
- Increasing skillet heat substantially produced noticeably more/larger holes. During experimentation the heat was eventually turned all the way up and hole formation improved.
- No flipping.
- A nonstick skillet worked without oil.

## What Worked

- Fermentation was visibly active.
- Batter could be successfully cooked and released from the nonstick pan.
- Absit could be incorporated successfully despite being overcooked somewhat.
- Adding 1 tbsp water improved the later injera.
- Higher pan heat clearly improved eye formation.
- Finished product was good enough to enjoy with Doro Wat and gomen.

## What Needs Improvement

- Much more sourness.
- Larger and more numerous open eyes.
- Lighter, more porous/spongy interior.
- Clearer absit endpoint.
- Better initial batter consistency.
- Better-defined skillet heat target.

## V2 Changes

1. Ferment 48 hours instead of about 24.
2. Keep the same 260 g teff / 475 g water / 1/4 tsp yeast base so fermentation duration can be evaluated cleanly.
3. Cook absit only until smooth and thick like pudding. Ignore translucency as a cue.
4. Start with a substantially hotter skillet.
5. Adjust final batter with water 1 tbsp at a time as needed.
6. Record sourness, eye size, internal porosity, flexibility, and any gumminess/density.
