# Instant Pot Gochujang Pork

Tender pork shoulder finished in a spicy-sweet Korean-inspired gochujang sauce, served over rice or tossed with Korean sweet potato noodles and vegetables. The pork is pressure-cooked before the gochujang is added so the sauce keeps its bright fermented flavor and can be adjusted at the end.

## Status

**Experimental**

## Yield

- Servings: 6 to 8
- Portion size: About 5 to 6 ounces cooked pork per person, plus rice or noodles and vegetables

## Required Equipment

- [Instant Pot](../../equipment/instant-pot.md)
- Cutting board and chef's knife
- Mixing bowl
- Tongs or two forks

## Helpful but Optional

- Large skillet or wok for vegetables and noodles
- Kitchen shears for shortening cooked sweet potato noodles
- Sheet pan and broiler for crisping the pork

## Ingredients

### Pork

- 3 1/2 to 4 pounds boneless pork shoulder or Boston butt
- 1 tablespoon Diamond Crystal kosher salt, or 2 teaspoons Morton kosher salt
- 1 teaspoon black pepper
- 1 tablespoon neutral cooking oil, if searing
- 1 medium onion, sliced
- 5 cloves garlic, minced
- 1 tablespoon finely grated fresh ginger
- 1 cup low-sodium chicken stock or water
- 1 tablespoon soy sauce

### Gochujang Sauce

- 1/4 cup gochujang
- 3 tablespoons soy sauce
- 2 tablespoons packed brown sugar or honey
- 1 tablespoon rice vinegar
- 2 teaspoons toasted sesame oil
- 1 teaspoon fish sauce, optional
- 1/4 to 1/2 cup reserved pork cooking liquid, as needed
- Gochugaru or crushed red pepper, optional, for additional heat

### Rice Option

- 2 cups uncooked jasmine rice, short-grain rice, or other preferred rice
- Water according to the rice package or rice-cooker instructions

### Sweet Potato Noodle Option

- 12 ounces Korean sweet potato starch noodles, also called dangmyeon
- 1 teaspoon toasted sesame oil, for holding the cooked noodles

### Vegetables

Use approximately 6 cups total. A mixture of the following works well:

- 1/2 small cabbage, thinly sliced
- 1 large broccoli crown, cut into small florets; peel and slice the stalk if using
- 1 bell pepper, thinly sliced
- 1 medium carrot, cut into thin matchsticks or coins
- 8 ounces mushrooms, sliced
- 1 tablespoon neutral cooking oil
- 1 to 2 tablespoons soy sauce, to taste

### Garnish

- Sliced green onions
- Toasted sesame seeds
- Kimchi, optional
- Fried or soft-boiled eggs, optional

## Method

1. **Cut and season the pork.** Trim only thick, hard exterior fat. Cut the pork into 3- to 4-inch chunks. Season evenly with the salt and black pepper.

2. **Brown the pork, optional but recommended.** Set the Instant Pot to Sauté on high. Add the neutral oil and brown the pork in batches for 2 to 3 minutes per broad side. Transfer the browned pork to a plate. Browning develops flavor, but the recipe will still work if this step is skipped.

3. **Cook the aromatics.** Reduce Sauté to normal. Add the onion and cook for 2 to 3 minutes. Add the garlic and ginger and cook for about 30 seconds, just until fragrant.

4. **Deglaze thoroughly.** Add the chicken stock and 1 tablespoon soy sauce. Scrape the bottom until no browned material remains stuck to the pot. This prevents a burn warning.

5. **Pressure-cook.** Return the pork and any accumulated juices to the pot. Lock the lid and cook on High Pressure for 70 minutes. Allow a 20-minute natural pressure release, then carefully vent the remaining pressure.

6. **Check tenderness.** Transfer one piece of pork to a plate and press it with a fork. It should separate with little resistance. If it remains firm or rubbery, pressure-cook for another 10 to 15 minutes and allow a 10-minute natural release.

7. **Reserve the liquid.** Transfer all pork to a bowl. Skim excess surface fat from the cooking liquid if practical. Reserve at least 1 cup of liquid; refrigerate or freeze any extra for soup, beans, rice, or reheating leftovers.

8. **Make the sauce.** In a bowl, whisk together the gochujang, 3 tablespoons soy sauce, brown sugar or honey, rice vinegar, sesame oil, and optional fish sauce. Whisk in 1/4 cup reserved cooking liquid. The sauce should be loose enough to coat the pork without becoming watery.

9. **Finish the pork.** Shred the pork into large bite-sized pieces rather than fine threads. Return it to the empty Instant Pot on Sauté low, add the sauce, and fold gently until evenly coated and hot, about 3 to 5 minutes. Add more cooking liquid a tablespoon at a time if the sauce becomes too thick. Taste and adjust with additional vinegar, sugar, soy sauce, or heat.

10. **Prepare the chosen base.**
    - **Rice:** Cook according to the package or rice-cooker instructions. Serve the pork and vegetables over the rice.
    - **Sweet potato noodles:** Cook according to the package instructions, usually by boiling for 6 to 8 minutes. Drain, rinse briefly under cool water, and toss with 1 teaspoon sesame oil. Cut the noodles several times with kitchen shears if they are difficult to serve.

11. **Cook the vegetables.** Heat a large skillet or wok over medium-high heat. Add 1 tablespoon neutral oil. Cook firm vegetables such as carrots and broccoli first, followed by mushrooms, cabbage, and bell pepper. Keep the vegetables slightly crisp. Season lightly with soy sauce.

12. **Assemble.**
    - **With rice:** Divide rice among bowls and top with vegetables, gochujang pork, green onions, and sesame seeds.
    - **With noodles:** Add the cooked noodles to the vegetables. Add enough pork and sauce to coat everything without flooding the pan. Toss over medium heat for 1 to 2 minutes, then garnish.

13. **Optional crisp finish.** Spread some sauced pork on a sheet pan and broil for 2 to 4 minutes until the edges darken and crisp. Watch closely because gochujang and sugar can burn quickly.

## Quality Targets

- Appearance: Glossy red-brown sauce clinging to the pork; vegetables remain visibly distinct
- Texture: Pork separates easily but retains meaty pieces; noodles are chewy rather than mushy; vegetables are tender-crisp
- Flavor: Savory, fermented, lightly sweet, tangy, and moderately spicy
- Doneness: Pork is fully tender and shreds with little resistance; tenderness matters more than a specific final temperature

## Notes

- Substitutions: Honey can replace brown sugar. Apple cider vinegar can replace rice vinegar. A small grated apple or Asian pear may be added to the sauce for extra sweetness.
- Heat level: Gochujang varies by brand. Begin with 1/4 cup and add more only after tasting the finished sauce.
- Timing considerations: Total Instant Pot time is approximately 2 hours after including searing, pressurization, cooking, and pressure release.
- Storage: Refrigerate pork, vegetables, and rice or noodles separately when practical for up to 4 days.
- Reheating: Warm pork gently with a spoonful of reserved cooking liquid. Noodles may need a splash of water before reheating.
- Freezing: The pork freezes well for up to 3 months. Rice also freezes well. The cooked noodles and vegetables are better refrigerated than frozen.

## Variables to Record

- Exact pork weight and chunk size
- Whether the pork was seared
- Pressure-cook time and release time
- Tenderness after the initial 70-minute cook
- Brand and heat level of gochujang
- Final amount of reserved cooking liquid added to the sauce
- Rice or noodle option used
- Vegetable mixture and cooking time
- Whether the pork was broiled after saucing
