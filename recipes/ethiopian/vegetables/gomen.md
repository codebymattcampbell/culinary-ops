# Gomen

Ethiopian-style collard greens cooked with onion, garlic, ginger, warm spices, butter, and lemon. Designed as a mild, savory counterpoint to spicy Doro Wat.

## Status

**Tested**

## Yield

- Servings: 3-4

## Ingredients

- 1 bunch collard greens
- 2 tbsp unsalted butter
- 1 medium yellow onion, diced
- 3 garlic cloves, minced
- 1 tbsp fresh ginger, grated or minced
- 1/2 tsp ground cardamom
- 1/4 tsp ground cumin
- 1/4 tsp black pepper
- 1/2 tsp kosher salt, plus more to taste
- 1/2 cup chicken stock
- 1 tbsp fresh lemon juice
- 1/2 jalapeño, finely minced, optional

## Method

1. Remove the thick center stems from the collards. Stack and roll the leaves, then slice into roughly 1/2-inch ribbons.
2. Melt 2 tbsp unsalted butter in a large skillet or pot over medium heat.
3. Add the diced onion and 1/2 tsp kosher salt. Cook 5-7 minutes, stirring occasionally, until softened.
4. Add the garlic, ginger, cardamom, cumin, black pepper, and optional jalapeño. Cook 30-60 seconds until fragrant.
5. Add the collards and toss through the seasoned butter and onions until they begin to wilt.
6. Pour in 1/2 cup chicken stock. Cover, reduce to medium-low, and cook about 15 minutes.
7. Uncover and cook another 5-10 minutes, stirring occasionally, until the collards are tender and most of the liquid has reduced.
8. Stir in 1 tbsp lemon juice. Taste and adjust salt.

## Quality Targets

- Tender collards that retain some structure rather than becoming mushy
- Little excess liquid remaining
- Buttery, gingery, savory flavor with warm spice
- Mild enough to balance a spicy main dish

## Notes

- Tested 20 Sep 2026 as part of an Ethiopian dinner with Doro Wat and teff injera and received very good reviews.
- Keep the jalapeño optional when serving with a heavily spiced Doro Wat.
