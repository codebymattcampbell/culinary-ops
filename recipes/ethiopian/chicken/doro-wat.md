# Doro Wat

A rich Ethiopian chicken stew built around deeply cooked onions, berbere spice, ginger, garlic, and butter. This tested variation uses boneless skinless chicken thighs and unsalted butter in place of bone-in thighs and niter kibbeh.

## Status

**Tested**

## Yield

- Servings: 4-6
- Portion size, when useful: 1-2 chicken thighs plus plenty of sauce

## Required Equipment

- Heavy Dutch oven, large heavy-bottomed pot, or Instant Pot used in Sauté mode
- Cutting board and knife
- Wooden spoon or heat-safe spatula
- Instant-read thermometer

## Helpful but Optional

- Food processor for finely chopping onions
- Injera for serving

## Ingredients

### Stew
- 2-2 1/2 lb boneless skinless chicken thighs
- 2 lb yellow onions, about 4 large, very finely chopped
- 4 tbsp unsalted butter, divided
- 3 tbsp berbere spice, plus more to taste
- 5 garlic cloves, minced
- 1 tbsp fresh ginger, finely grated
- 2 tbsp tomato paste
- 1 1/2 cups low-sodium chicken stock, divided; use as needed
- 2 tbsp fresh lemon juice, plus more to taste
- 1 tsp kosher salt, divided, plus more to taste
- 1/4 tsp ground cardamom
- 1/4 tsp ground cumin
- 1/8 tsp ground cinnamon
- Pinch ground cloves

### Optional Traditional Addition
- 6 hard-boiled eggs, peeled and lightly scored

### For Serving
- Injera
- Plain yogurt, optional for cooling the heat

## Method

1. Pat the chicken thighs dry. Toss with 2 tbsp lemon juice and 1/2 tsp kosher salt. Leave whole unless unusually large.
2. Place the finely chopped onions in a dry heavy pot over medium-low heat. Cook, stirring frequently, until they release their moisture, collapse dramatically, and become golden and nearly jammy, about 25-35 minutes. Add a small splash of water if needed to prevent scorching.
3. Add 3 tbsp unsalted butter, cardamom, cumin, cinnamon, and cloves. Cook 3-4 minutes, stirring.
4. Add the berbere, garlic, and ginger. Cook about 2 minutes, stirring constantly. Add a splash of stock if the spices threaten to scorch.
5. Add the tomato paste and cook 2-3 minutes until it darkens slightly.
6. Add 1 cup chicken stock and scrape the bottom thoroughly. Bring to a gentle simmer.
7. Nestle the boneless thighs into the sauce. Cover and simmer gently for about 15 minutes. Do not pressure cook.
8. Uncover, turn the thighs, and continue at a gentle simmer until the thickest pieces reach about 170-175°F and are tender, generally another 10-15 minutes. Stir around the chicken frequently. Add stock a few tablespoons at a time if the sauce becomes too thick before the chicken is done.
9. If using hard-boiled eggs, score each with 3-4 shallow cuts and add during the final 10 minutes.
10. Turn off the heat and stir in the remaining 1 tbsp unsalted butter.
11. Taste and adjust with salt, lemon juice, or additional berbere as desired.
12. Rest about 10 minutes before serving.

## Quality Targets

- Appearance: Thick, brick-red to dark reddish-brown sauce that clings to the chicken.
- Texture: Boneless thighs tender and juicy; onions nearly disappeared into the sauce.
- Flavor: Deeply savory, spicy, onion-rich, buttery, aromatic, and balanced with acidity.
- Doneness: Boneless thighs about 170-175°F in the thickest portion, with tenderness taking priority.

## Notes

- 20 Sep 2026 test used boneless skinless thighs, unsalted butter, and no eggs and received very good reviews.
- The Instant Pot works well as the cooking vessel in Sauté mode, but this version is not pressure cooked.
- On the tested Instant Pot, the lowest custom Sauté setting did not maintain a simmer while the next setting was fairly aggressive. Adjust between settings as needed and stir frequently.
- Berbere heat varies significantly by brand.
- The long onion reduction remains the foundation of the sauce.
- Traditional niter kibbeh can replace the butter and supplemental cardamom/cumin/cinnamon/clove mixture.

## Variables to Record

- Berbere brand and exact amount
- Perceived heat level
- Onion weight and reduction time
- Butter vs niter kibbeh
- Chicken thigh weight
- Final sauce thickness
- Salt and acidity balance
- Egg vs no-egg preference
