# Peri-Peri Spatchcock Chicken

A hot-and-fast kamado chicken with crisp skin, smoky chipotle peri-peri flavor, and a juicy breast. This version uses a baking-powder dry brine plus a very thin peri-peri-mayo coating to improve browning without creating a heavy paste layer.

## Status

**Experimental**

## Yield

- Servings: 3-4
- Portion size, when useful: 1/4 to 1/3 chicken per person

## Required Equipment

- Small ceramic Egg / kamado
- Heat deflector for indirect cooking
- Instant-read thermometer
- Kitchen shears
- Wire rack and sheet pan
- Blender or food processor

## Helpful but Optional

- Pecan chips or a small hickory chunk for smoke

## Ingredients

### Chicken
- 1 whole chicken, about 4-5 lb
- 1 1/2 tsp kosher salt
- 1 tsp aluminum-free baking powder

### Chipotle Peri-Peri Sauce
- 2 chipotle chiles in adobo
- 1 tbsp adobo sauce
- 4 garlic cloves
- 2 tbsp lemon juice
- 1 tbsp red wine vinegar
- 1 tsp smoked paprika
- 1 tsp dried oregano
- 1/2 tsp ground cumin
- 1 tsp tomato paste
- 2 tbsp olive oil
- Black pepper to taste

### Skin Coating
- 2 tbsp peri-peri sauce
- 1 tbsp mayonnaise

### Finish
- Reserved clean peri-peri sauce
- Fresh lemon juice, to taste

## Method

1. Spatchcock the chicken by cutting along both sides of the backbone with kitchen shears. Remove the backbone, flip the chicken skin-side up, and press firmly on the breastbone until the bird lies flat.
2. Pat the chicken very dry, especially the skin.
3. Mix the kosher salt and baking powder. Sprinkle evenly over the chicken, focusing especially on the skin side.
4. Place the chicken uncovered on a wire rack in the refrigerator for 45-60 minutes. Longer is acceptable if convenient.
5. Make the peri-peri sauce by blending the chipotle chiles, adobo sauce, garlic, lemon juice, red wine vinegar, smoked paprika, oregano, cumin, tomato paste, olive oil, and black pepper until fairly smooth.
6. Divide the sauce into a raw-chicken portion and a clean finishing portion. Keep the finishing portion completely separate from anything that touches raw poultry.
7. Set up the Egg for indirect cooking with the heat deflector. Target 375-400°F dome temperature, ideally near 400°F.
8. If using smoke wood, add a small handful of pecan chips. One small hickory chunk may be added for a slightly stronger smoke profile. Wait for heavy white smoke to settle before cooking.
9. Remove the chicken from the refrigerator. If the skin has visible surface moisture, pat it gently dry.
10. Mix 2 tbsp peri-peri sauce with 1 tbsp mayonnaise. Spread a very thin film over the skin. The skin texture should still be clearly visible through the coating.
11. Brush the underside/meat side lightly with plain peri-peri sauce. Avoid a heavy coating.
12. Place the chicken on the Egg skin-side up, indirect, with the legs oriented toward the hotter side of the cooker.
13. Cook without disturbing for about 30 minutes, then check the deepest part of the breast and the thickest part of the thigh, avoiding bone.
14. Continue cooking until the coolest part of the breast reaches 155-157°F and the thighs are preferably 170°F or higher.
15. If the meat is nearly done but the skin needs more color, finish briefly over direct heat skin-side down for 30-60 seconds at a time, watching carefully to avoid scorching the garlic and chile solids.
16. Rest the chicken uncovered for 10-15 minutes.
17. Finish with a small amount of the reserved clean peri-peri sauce and fresh lemon juice. Taste before adding additional salt.

## Quality Targets

- Appearance: Deep golden to reddish-brown skin with light blistering and some darker smoke-roasted spots, but no heavy black scorching.
- Texture: Skin should be dry and crisp rather than rubbery; breast should remain juicy; thighs should be tender.
- Flavor: Smoky, garlicky, acidic, moderately hot, and savory. Chipotle will make this version smokier and less traditional than classic peri-peri.
- Doneness or temperature: Pull breast at 155-157°F in the coolest spot; thighs preferably 170°F+; rest 10-15 minutes uncovered.

## Notes

- Substitutions: Fresh red chiles can replace chipotle chiles for a brighter, more traditional peri-peri profile. If using fresh chiles, adjust heat level to taste.
- Timing considerations: A 4-5 lb spatchcock chicken should generally take about 40-55 minutes at 400°F indirect, but temperature determines doneness.
- Storage and reheating: Refrigerate leftovers promptly. Reheat uncovered in a hot oven or air fryer when possible to preserve skin texture.

## Variables to Record

Use this section while the recipe is still experimental.

- Exact chicken weight
- Actual stabilized dome temperature
- Total cook time
- Breast temperature at pull
- Thigh temperature at pull
- Carryover temperature during rest
- Skin crispness
- Smoke intensity
- Heat level
- Acid balance
- Whether mayo improved or softened the skin
- Whether pecan alone provided enough smoke
