# Doro Wat

A rich Ethiopian chicken stew built around deeply cooked onions, berbere spice, ginger, garlic, and clarified butter, with hard-boiled eggs added near the end. This version is designed as a practical first pass using ingredients that are reasonably easy to source while preserving the dish's characteristic deep, spicy, savory flavor.

## Status

**Experimental**

## Yield

- Servings: 4-6
- Portion size, when useful: 1-2 chicken thighs plus sauce and 1 egg per person

## Required Equipment

- Heavy Dutch oven or large heavy-bottomed pot with lid
- Cutting board and knife
- Wooden spoon or heat-safe spatula
- Instant-read thermometer
- Small saucepan for eggs

## Helpful but Optional

- Food processor for finely chopping onions
- Injera for serving

## Ingredients

### Stew
- 6 bone-in, skinless chicken thighs, about 2 1/2-3 lb total
- 2 lb yellow onions, about 4 large, very finely chopped
- 4 tbsp niter kibbeh (Ethiopian spiced clarified butter), divided
- 3 tbsp berbere spice, plus more to taste
- 5 garlic cloves, minced
- 1 tbsp fresh ginger, finely grated
- 2 tbsp tomato paste
- 1 1/2 cups low-sodium chicken stock, plus more if needed
- 2 tbsp fresh lemon juice
- 1 tsp kosher salt, plus more to taste
- 6 large eggs

### If You Do Not Have Niter Kibbeh
- 4 tbsp ghee or unsalted butter
- 1/4 tsp ground cardamom
- 1/4 tsp ground cumin
- 1/8 tsp ground cinnamon
- Pinch ground cloves

### For Serving
- Injera, if available
- Basmati rice as an easy alternative
- Plain yogurt, optional for cooling the heat

## Method

1. Pat the chicken thighs dry. Toss with the lemon juice and 1/2 tsp kosher salt. Set aside while the onions begin cooking.
2. Place the finely chopped onions in a dry Dutch oven over medium-low heat. Cook, stirring frequently, until they release their moisture, soften dramatically, and begin turning golden, about 25-35 minutes. Do not rush this stage; the onions form the body of the sauce.
3. Add 3 tbsp niter kibbeh. If substituting ghee or butter, add it with the cardamom, cumin, cinnamon, and cloves. Cook for 3-4 minutes, stirring continuously.
4. Add the berbere, garlic, and ginger. Cook for about 2 minutes, stirring constantly, until very fragrant. If the spices begin sticking or scorching, add a small splash of stock.
5. Add the tomato paste and cook for 2-3 minutes, stirring, until it darkens slightly.
6. Add 1 cup of the chicken stock and scrape the bottom of the pot thoroughly. Bring to a gentle simmer.
7. Nestle the chicken thighs into the sauce. Cover and simmer gently for 20 minutes.
8. Meanwhile, place the eggs in a saucepan, cover with cold water, and bring to a boil. Remove from the heat, cover, and let stand for 10-11 minutes. Transfer to cold water, cool, and peel.
9. Uncover the stew and turn the chicken thighs. Continue simmering uncovered for another 20-30 minutes, stirring around the chicken occasionally, until the sauce is thick and the chicken is very tender. Add the remaining stock as needed if the sauce becomes too thick before the chicken is done.
10. Check the chicken in the thickest part away from the bone. For this stew, target at least 175°F so the thighs become tender rather than merely cooked through.
11. Lightly score each peeled egg with 3-4 shallow cuts so the sauce can flavor the surface. Add the eggs to the stew for the final 10 minutes of cooking.
12. Stir in the remaining 1 tbsp niter kibbeh. Taste and adjust with additional salt, berbere, or lemon juice as needed.
13. Let the stew rest off heat for 10 minutes before serving. Spoon plenty of sauce over each portion of chicken and egg.

## Quality Targets

- Appearance: Thick, brick-red to dark reddish-brown sauce that clings to the chicken rather than running like broth.
- Texture: Chicken thighs should be very tender; onions should have nearly disappeared into the sauce; eggs should remain firm but not rubbery.
- Flavor: Deeply savory, warm, spicy, onion-rich, buttery, and aromatic, with enough acidity to keep the stew from tasting heavy.
- Doneness or temperature: Chicken thighs at least 175°F in the thickest part, with tenderness taking priority over the exact final number.

## Notes

- Substitutions: Boneless skinless thighs will work but will cook faster and produce a slightly less rich stew. Avoid chicken breast for the first version because it is less forgiving during the long simmer.
- Berbere heat varies significantly by brand. Start with 2 tbsp if the blend is known to be very hot, then add more after tasting.
- The onion stage matters more than almost anything else. Finely chopped onions cooked slowly until soft and concentrated create the characteristic body of the sauce without flour or starch.
- Traditional versions commonly use niter kibbeh. The seasoned ghee/butter substitute above is not identical but will move the flavor in the right direction for a first test.
- Timing considerations: Plan on about 75-90 minutes total, including onion cooking and simmering.
- Storage and reheating: Refrigerate up to 4 days. The flavor often improves overnight. Reheat gently with a splash of stock or water if the sauce has tightened too much.
