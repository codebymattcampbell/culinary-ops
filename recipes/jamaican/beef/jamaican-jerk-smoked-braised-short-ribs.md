# Jamaican Jerk Smoked-Braised Short Ribs

Jerk-seasoned beef short ribs smoked on the Egg, then covered-braised until probe tender. The braising liquid is finished as a rich jus for serving.

## Status

**Tested**

## Yield

- Servings: 3-4
- Beef: about 2.2 lb cross-cut/flanken-style beef short ribs

## Required Equipment

- [Big Green Egg](../../../equipment/egg.md) or other kamado/smoker
- Foil pan
- Heavy-duty aluminum foil
- Instant-read thermometer or probe
- Small saucepan for finishing the jus

## Ingredients

### Beef
- 2.2 lb beef short ribs, cross-cut/flanken style
- 2 tbsp Walker's Wood Traditional Jamaican Jerk Seasoning, Hot & Spicy

### Braising Liquid
- 1 cup beef stock
- 1/2 cup water
- 2 tbsp lime juice
- 1 tbsp brown sugar
- 1 tbsp Walker's Wood Traditional Jamaican Jerk Seasoning, Hot & Spicy
- 1 tsp Worcestershire sauce
- 1 tsp thyme leaves
- 3 garlic cloves, minced
- 1/2 medium yellow onion, thinly sliced
- 1/4 tsp ground allspice

## Method

1. Coat the short ribs with about 2 tbsp jerk seasoning. Refrigerate for several hours.
2. Set up the Egg for indirect cooking and stabilize around 250-275°F.
3. Smoke the ribs uncovered until they develop a dark reddish-brown exterior, about 45-75 minutes. Judge this stage by color rather than internal temperature.
4. Combine all braising-liquid ingredients in a foil pan.
5. Place the ribs in the pan bones down. The liquid should reach roughly 1/3-1/2 of the way up the meat. Add a little stock or water only if needed.
6. Cover the pan tightly with foil and return it to the Egg. Let the cooker run around 275-300°F. It can be raised to 325-350°F near the end if the ribs need to finish faster.
7. Begin checking after about 45-60 minutes of braising. Pull individual pieces as they become probe tender. Temperature will often be around 195-205°F, but tenderness is the deciding factor.
8. Rest the ribs covered for 10-15 minutes.
9. Pour the braising liquid into a saucepan and skim excess fat. Taste before reducing. If needed, simmer uncovered 5-10 minutes until lightly glossy and concentrated.
10. Adjust the finished jus with a small squeeze of lime if it needs brightness. Spoon over the ribs or serve alongside.

## Quality Targets

- Exterior: Dark jerk-seasoned surface with clear smoke character
- Texture: Probe slides into the meat with very little resistance
- Flavor: Savory, smoky, aromatic jerk flavor with moderate chile heat
- Jus: Beefy and gelatin-rich without becoming excessively salty from reduction

## Notes

- The tested heat level was judged just right; no additional Scotch bonnet mash was needed in the braise.
- Cross-cut ribs cook faster than thick English-cut short ribs. Use tenderness rather than a fixed braise time.
- The covered braise is the critical tenderizing stage. Once covered, maintaining a low smoking temperature is no longer necessary.
- The braising liquid is worth keeping and finishing as jus.

## Variables to Record

- Rib cut and thickness
- Jerk seasoning amount
- Smoke time and dome temperature
- Braise time and temperature
- Probe-tender temperature by piece
- Jus reduction time
- Final salt, acidity, and heat
