# Garlic-Lime Tostones

Twice-cooked green plantains with a quick garlic, salt, and lime dip between cooks. The tested Blackstone cook showed that direct griddle contact can be more reliable than trying to heat a pan of oil through the griddle surface.

## Status

**Experimental**

## Yield

- Servings: 3
- Plantains: 3 green plantains

## Required Equipment

- [Blackstone](../../equipment/blackstone.md) or stovetop burner and sturdy frying pan
- Tongs or slotted spoon
- Tostonera or sturdy flat object for smashing
- Thermometer if deep/shallow frying

## Ingredients

### Plantains
- 3 green plantains
- Neutral high-heat oil, as needed
- Kosher salt, to finish

### Garlic-Lime Dip
- 1/2 cup water
- 2 garlic cloves, crushed or very finely minced
- 1/2 tsp kosher salt
- 1 tsp lime juice

## Method

1. Mix the water, garlic, salt, and lime juice and let it stand while preparing the plantains.
2. Cut the ends from the plantains, score the peels lengthwise, and remove the peels.
3. Cut the plantains crosswise into pieces about 1 inch thick.
4. First cook until the centers are fully softened enough to smash without crumbling. When frying, target about 325°F and use tenderness rather than a fixed time; 5-7 minutes may be required depending on oil recovery and piece size.
5. Smash the hot pieces to roughly 1/4-1/3 inch thick.
6. Give each smashed plantain a very quick dip in the garlic-lime water. Drain and shake off excess water thoroughly before it contacts hot oil or the griddle.
7. For traditional frying, fry again at 350-375°F until crisp and golden, about 2-3 minutes per batch.
8. For the Blackstone adaptation, oil the griddle generously and crisp the smashed plantains directly on the hot steel, flipping as needed until deeply golden and crisp.
9. Salt immediately and serve hot.

## Quality Targets

- Crisp, deeply golden exterior
- Tender interior that holds together rather than crumbling
- Light garlic-lime seasoning
- Not greasy or waterlogged

## Notes

- First-cook tenderness is the critical variable. If the pieces crumble while smashing, they were likely not softened enough through the center.
- During the first test, a pan of oil placed on the Blackstone recovered heat too slowly. Moving the plantains directly onto the Blackstone produced much better results; roughly half the batch finished very well.
- Do not soak the plantains in the garlic water. Use a quick dip and drain very well.
- If pieces crumble, they can still be crisped as irregular plantain chunks rather than discarded.

## Variables to Record

- Plantain ripeness
- Piece thickness
- First-cook method and duration
- Temperature or surface temperature
- Ease of smashing
- Traditional second fry vs direct Blackstone crisping
- Final crispness
