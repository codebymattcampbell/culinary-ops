# Jamaican-Style Collard Greens

Collard greens cooked with onion, scallion, tomato, thyme, garlic, and allspice as a practical substitute for callaloo.

## Status

**Tested**

## Yield

- Servings: 3-4

## Required Equipment

- Large skillet or pot with lid
- Cutting board and knife

## Ingredients

- 1 large bunch collard greens
- 1 tbsp butter or neutral oil
- 1/2 medium yellow onion, thinly sliced
- 2 scallions, sliced
- 2 garlic cloves, minced
- 1 medium tomato, diced
- 1/2 tsp thyme leaves
- 1/4 tsp ground allspice
- 1/2 tsp kosher salt, plus more to taste
- Black pepper, to taste
- 1/4 cup water, chicken stock, or reserved pigeon-pea cooking liquid
- Up to 1/8 tsp Scotch bonnet mash, optional

## Method

1. Strip out the thick center stems from the collards and slice the leaves into roughly 1/2-inch ribbons.
2. Heat the butter or oil over medium heat.
3. Add the onion and scallions and cook 3-4 minutes until beginning to soften.
4. Add the garlic, thyme, allspice, and optional Scotch bonnet mash. Cook about 30 seconds.
5. Add the tomato and cook about 2 minutes.
6. Add the collards, liquid, salt, and black pepper. Cover and cook 20-25 minutes, stirring once or twice.
7. Uncover and cook about 5 minutes longer to reduce excess liquid.
8. Taste and adjust salt and pepper before serving.

## Quality Targets

- Greens tender without becoming mushy
- Savory onion, garlic, thyme, and tomato flavor
- Enough liquid to cook the greens without leaving them soupy
- Heat supportive rather than dominant

## Notes

- This is a collard-green adaptation, not traditional Jamaican callaloo.
- Reserved pigeon-pea cooking liquid works well in place of water or stock and adds a little body.
- Keep the greens relatively light when serving with rich jerk-braised beef and coconut rice.

## Variables to Record

- Collard bunch weight
- Cooking time to preferred tenderness
- Liquid amount
- Scotch bonnet amount
- Final salt level
