# Japanese Soy-Marinated Ramen Eggs (Ajitsuke Tamago)

Soy-seasoned whites with creamy, jammy centers. This is an independent recipe with no required soup pairing.

## Status

**Experimental. Planned, not confirmed cooked.** No egg texture, marinade flavor or finished result was recorded.

## Yield

- 5 eggs
- Prep: approximately 5 minutes; boil: 7 minutes; ice bath: 10 minutes; marination: approximately 8 hours. All are planned timings.

## Required Equipment

- Large saucepan and stove
- Slotted spoon and timer
- Bowl for an ice bath
- Small airtight food-safe container or suitable food-safe bag
- Refrigerator

## Ingredients

- 5 large refrigerator-cold eggs
- ¼ cup low-sodium soy sauce
- ½ cup water
- 2 tablespoons mirin
- 1 teaspoon rice vinegar
- 2 thin slices fresh ginger
- 1 teaspoon sugar, optional
- Water for boiling; ice and water for cooling

The marinade was originally proposed for six eggs. For five, use a small enough container or bag for good coverage.

## Method

1. Fill a saucepan with enough water to cover eggs by about 1 inch. Bring to a full boil and prepare an ice bath.
2. Carefully lower all five cold eggs into the water. Start a **7-minute timer once all eggs are submerged**. Maintain a steady, controlled boil.
3. Transfer immediately to the ice bath for about **10 minutes**.
4. Gently crack and peel, handling the soft whites carefully.
5. Combine soy sauce, water, mirin, vinegar, ginger and optional sugar. If using alcohol-containing mirin, the plan calls for a brief simmer, then complete cooling. A brief simmer does not guarantee removal of all alcohol.
6. Put peeled eggs in the cooled marinade. Refrigerate about **8 hours**, turning occasionally if not fully submerged.
7. Remove when the desired flavor develops, transfer to a clean airtight container, and discard used marinade.

## Quality Targets

- Seasoned whites and a jammy center with clean peeling.
- Proposed timing references: **6½ minutes** for softer/runny, **7 minutes** for jammy, **8 minutes** for firmer/creamy, **10–12 minutes** for fully set.
- These are starting estimates, not tested results or safety guarantees. Egg size, altitude and water-temperature recovery affect doneness; check the actual result.

## Notes

- The five-egg batch, seven-minute target and eight-hour marinade were the v1 plan.
- Proposed marination comparison: 4 hours lighter, 8 hours preferred starting point, 12 hours stronger. Longer soaking can make whites salty and firm; these outcomes remain untested here.
- Serve halved over ramen, rice or noodle bowls, or alongside breakfast. Avoid prolonged boiling after marination if preserving the jammy center.
- Marination adds flavor; it does not pasteurize eggs or make them shelf-stable.

## Storage and Food Safety

- Jammy yolks are partially cooked. FDA guidance is to cook both whites and yolks firm; use fully cooked eggs for people at elevated risk of foodborne illness.
- Keep eggs and marinade refrigerated at **40°F / 4°C or below**.
- The original plan proposed using refrigerated jammy eggs within **1–2 days** as a conservative household limit. This is not an official validated safe-storage period for undercooked eggs.
- Fully hard-cooked eggs are the safer option for longer meal preparation; refrigerate within two hours and use within **one week of cooking**. Do not rely solely on a timer to confirm firm yolks.
- [FDA egg safety guidance](https://www.fda.gov/media/82227/download)

- Yolk texture and peeling
- Marinade strength at eight hours; effect of vinegar
- Fully cooked variation for longer breakfast preparation
